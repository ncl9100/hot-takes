# Judging Criteria and Source Inventory

## 1. Hackathon judging rubric (transcribed from Nathan's screenshots, 2026-10-03)

Each category is scored 4 (Excellent), 3 (Good), 2 (Satisfactory) or 1 (Needs Improvement). Five categories visible, so 20 points max if equally weighted (weights not stated).

| Category | What is judged | 4 points (Excellent) |
|---|---|---|
| Technology | How advanced and effective the use of technology is | Cutting-edge or superbly applied to the problem; high complexity and sophistication |
| Presentation | Clarity, engagement, professionalism | Clear, engaging, professional; communicates the project's value and workings |
| Innovation / Creativity | Originality of the idea and creative problem-solving | Novel approach and creative problem-solving with exceptional outcomes |
| Execution | Quality of implementation and how well the project functions | Exceptionally well implemented, flawless functionality, polished finish |
| Theme | Relevance to and integration of the hackathon theme | Theme incorporated in a meaningful and integral way |

Lower bands: 3 = effective/clear but not especially innovative or engaging; 2 = adequate but lacking complexity, clarity or originality; 1 = basic, unclear, derivative or barely on theme.

Open questions: whether there are more rows below "Theme" (screenshot shows a scroll control); category weights; whether Grundfos/HDR judges also score against the challenge brief's four areas.

## 2. Challenge brief evaluation areas (from NYU_Hackathon_Data_Center_Heat_Reuse_Challenge_R1)

- Technical: capture point, temperature and capacity; heat pumps / thermal upgrading; reliability, timing and continuity
- Economic + Delivery: capital and operating costs; revenue / connection models; ownership, risks and responsibilities
- Environmental: carbon reduction; energy efficiency; resource optimization
- Social + Regenerative: measurable stakeholder value; equity and public acceptance; local-context fit

Deliverable: concise, evidence-based system proposal for ONE chosen site (111 8th Ave urban, or Lake Hawkeye suburban), covering architecture, heat source and users, infrastructure, operating approach, supply/demand matching (temperature, capacity, timing, seasonality, continuity), cooling reliability protection, quantified value, and cost/risk/ownership sharing.

## 3. Source access

All text sources readable. Not readable: Williams_College_and_the_Geothermal_Earth_Battery.m4a (audio, no transcript). Image-only content: site PDF maps, Topic 5 configuration diagrams.
